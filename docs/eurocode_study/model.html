5. The probabilistic model#

Step 4 of the procedure: the failure modes and the probabilistic models of the variables, as agreed in the Ad-hoc Group (input of November 2021, [Köhler et al., 2021]). The tables on this page are generated from codecal.ahg_spec() — the model as the code holds it — so they cannot drift from what the tool computes. The same numbers appear in the Model tab of the lab mode.

Conventions. All variables are normalised to mean 1 (except the resistance and load model uncertainties, whose mean expresses a bias), so the model is a table of coefficients of variation, characteristic values and weights:

  • Resistance model uncertainties \(\Theta_R\) are lognormal; their characteristic value is 1 by definition. A mean of 1.15 means that the real capacity is, on average, 15 % larger than the model predicts.

  • Material strengths are lognormal with the 5 % fractile as characteristic value — except steel and aluminium, whose characteristic values were assigned by the experts of the group (0.83 and 0.85, corresponding to the 0.01 % and 0.06 % fractiles), following the nominal values of EN 1993-1-1 Annex E [CEN, 2005].

  • Self-weights are normal with the mean (or a pinned value slightly below it) as characteristic value; the other permanent load \(G_P\) (called \(P\) in the code) is normal with CoV 0.10 and the mean as characteristic value.

  • Variable loads are represented by the 50-year maximum (Gumbel, mean 1) with a basic period of 1 year (wind, snow) or 5 years (imposed). The characteristic value is the 98 % fractile (99 % for the imposed load) over the basic period of the product \(\Theta_Q \cdot Q\), obtained by Monte-Carlo simulation. For the imposed load the simulation would give 1.38; the value 1.35 was conventionally assigned in the study.

  • \(\Theta_E\), the model uncertainty of the load effect, is lognormal with CoV 0.10 and characteristic value 1.

  • The load proportions \(a_Q\) and \(a_G\) are varied uniformly between the limits per failure mode (10 values of \(a_Q\), 3 of \(a_G\)); the weight of a design situation is \(w_{\text{mode}} \, w_{\text{load}} / (n_{a_G} n_{a_Q})\). The weights are expert judgement of how frequent the situations are; the results are moderately sensitive to them.

    """'0.83 (pinned)' or '5% fractile' for a random-variable entry of the spec."""

5.6. Modes not in the default set#

The 2021 input also listed buckling modes with weight zero or very small weight (steel, glulam, timber and aluminium buckling, with Θ_R CoV 0.10–0.15 and weights 0 / 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.01). They are not part of the default model; they can be added in the lab mode or in a JSON model file (Model files (JSON)).
