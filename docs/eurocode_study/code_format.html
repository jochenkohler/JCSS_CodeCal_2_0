4. The code format: three combination rules#

The study in one paragraph

During the revision of the Eurocodes, members of the JCSS prepared the scientific background for a possible reliability-based re-calibration of the partial factors, within the CEN/TC 250/SC 10 Ad-hoc Group Reliability Background of the Eurocodes [Vrouwenvelder et al., 2024]. The work followed the seven steps of Reliability-based code calibration, with the emphasis on steps 3–7: the code format (this page), the failure modes and probabilistic models (The probabilistic model), the measure of closeness, the optimal factors and the verification (Results of the Eurocode study — reproduced, Verification: how good is FORM here?). Sources: [Köhler, 2026, Köhler et al., 2021, Köhler et al., 2023].

4.1. Step 3 — the format: equations (8.12), (8.13) and (8.14)#

EN 1990:2023 [CEN, 2023] offers three alternative expressions for the combination of actions in persistent and transient design situations, (8.12), (8.13) and (8.14) — the successors of (6.10) and (6.10 a/b) of EN 1990:2002. National standardisation committees must choose one of them, and to support that choice the reliability implied by each was needed.

The design situations of the study comprise one permanent load (self-weight \(G_S\) and other permanent load \(G_P\)) and one variable load \(Q_j\). For such cases the three expressions become design equations in which the design solution \(p_{ij}\) — the resistance that the engineer provides for failure mode \(i\) under variable load \(j\), e.g. the cross-section area of a tension rod — is identified as follows.

(8.12) — one combination:

\[ p_{ij} = \frac{\gamma_{M,i}}{\theta_{Ri,k}\, r_{i,k}} \Big\{ (1-a_Q)\big[a_G\,\gamma_{GS}\,g_{S,k} + (1-a_G)\,\gamma_{GP}\,g_{P,k}\big] + a_Q\,\theta_{Qj,k}\,\gamma_Q\,q_{j,k} \Big\} \]

(8.13) — the larger of two combinations, the first with the variable load reduced by \(\psi_{0,j}\), the second with the permanent load reduced by \(\xi\):

\[\begin{split} p_{ij} = \max \begin{cases} \dfrac{\gamma_{M,i}}{\theta_{Ri,k}\, r_{i,k}} \Big\{ (1-a_Q)\big[a_G\,\gamma_{GS}\,g_{S,k} + (1-a_G)\,\gamma_{GP}\,g_{P,k}\big] + a_Q\,\theta_{Qj,k}\,\psi_{0,j}\,\gamma_Q\,q_{j,k} \Big\} \\[2ex] \dfrac{\gamma_{M,i}}{\theta_{Ri,k}\, r_{i,k}} \Big\{ (1-a_Q)\,\xi\big[a_G\,\gamma_{GS}\,g_{S,k} + (1-a_G)\,\gamma_{GP}\,g_{P,k}\big] + a_Q\,\theta_{Qj,k}\,\gamma_Q\,q_{j,k} \Big\} \end{cases} \end{split}\]

(8.14) — the larger of the permanent load alone and the second combination of (8.13):

\[\begin{split} p_{ij} = \max \begin{cases} \dfrac{\gamma_{M,i}}{\theta_{Ri,k}\, r_{i,k}} \,(1-a_Q)\big[a_G\,\gamma_{GS}\,g_{S,k} + (1-a_G)\,\gamma_{GP}\,g_{P,k}\big] \\[2ex] \dfrac{\gamma_{M,i}}{\theta_{Ri,k}\, r_{i,k}} \Big\{ (1-a_Q)\,\xi\big[a_G\,\gamma_{GS}\,g_{S,k} + (1-a_G)\,\gamma_{GP}\,g_{P,k}\big] + a_Q\,\theta_{Qj,k}\,\gamma_Q\,q_{j,k} \Big\} \end{cases} \end{split}\]

Three parts can be recognised in each: the resistance (the fraction in front), the permanent load (the term in square brackets) and the variable load (the term with \(q_{j,k}\)). The second sub-equations of (8.13) and (8.14) are identical; the first sub-equation of (8.14) neglects the variable load, and the first of (8.13) reduces it by \(\psi_0\). Since the second combination governs most situations, (8.13) and (8.14) give nearly the same designs — and both give smaller designs than (8.12).

4.1.1. Nomenclature#

\(p_{ij}\)

the design solution for material (failure mode) \(i\) and variable load \(j\)

\(\gamma_{M,i}\)

the material partial factor of failure mode \(i\) (fixed in the study)

\(\theta_{Ri,k}\), \(r_{i,k}\)

the characteristic values of the resistance model uncertainty (often 1; a bias or hidden safety could be introduced here) and of the material strength

\(a_Q\)

the share of the variable load in the total load, between 0 (no variable load) and 1 (no permanent load)

\(a_G\)

the share of the structural self-weight in the permanent load, between 0 (no self-weight) and 1 (no other permanent load)

\(g_{S,k}\), \(\gamma_{GS}\)

the characteristic value of the self-weight and its partial factor

\(g_{P,k}\), \(\gamma_{GP}\)

the characteristic value of the permanent load from non-structural elements and its partial factor

\(q_{j,k}\), \(\theta_{Qj,k}\), \(\gamma_Q\)

the characteristic value of variable load \(j\), of its model uncertainty, and the partial factor on the variable load

\(\psi_{0,j}\)

the combination factor of variable load \(j\)

\(\xi\)

the reduction factor on the permanent actions

In CodeCal 2.0, \(p_{ij}\) is called the design parameter \(z\) (DesignSituation.design_parameter), and \(\gamma_{GS}\), \(\gamma_{GP}\) are gamma_G and gamma_GP of PartialFactors. Following the convention of the study, calibrating “γ_G” calibrates both permanent-load factors together unless gamma_GP is named separately.

4.2. The limit state function#

For all three design equations the same limit state function applies,

(4.1)#\[ g(\mathbf{X}, p_{ij}) = p_{ij}\,\Theta_{R,i}\,R_i - \Theta_E \Big[ (1-a_Q)\big(a_G\,G_S + (1-a_G)\,G_P\big) + a_Q\,\Theta_{Q,j}\,Q_j \Big] , \]

with the seven random variables

\(\Theta_{R,i}\), \(R_i\)

the resistance model uncertainty and the strength of material \(i\)

\(\Theta_E\)

the model uncertainty of the load effect (common to all situations)

\(G_S\), \(G_P\)

the self-weight and the other permanent load

\(\Theta_{Q,j}\), \(Q_j\)

the model uncertainty and the 50-year maximum of variable load \(j\).

A design situation is thus fully described by a failure mode \(i\), a variable load \(j\), and the two proportions \((a_G, a_Q)\). The design equation gives \(p_{ij}\), the limit state function with that \(p_{ij}\) gives \(\beta\) by FORM, and the sweep over all situations gives the picture of the code.

4.3. The benchmark and the calibration#

Compliance with the reliability objective is assessed for the three rules on a representative domain \(D\) of design situations: nine failure modes, three variable loads, and the ranges of \(a_Q\) and \(a_G\) of the next page. Two steps:

  1. the design solutions \(p_{ij}\) are computed with (8.12), (8.13) or (8.14) and the present factors (\(\gamma_G = 1.35\), \(\gamma_Q = 1.5\), \(\xi = 0.85\), \(\psi_0\) = 0.6 / 0.5 / 0.7 for wind / snow / imposed);

  2. the reliability index of each solution is computed with the limit state function (4.1).

The weighted average \(\beta_{\text{w-average}} = \sum_k w_k \beta_k\) is the headline number; the spread of the \(\beta_k\) is the story. For the calibration, an alternative set of action factors \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_S = (\gamma_G, \gamma_{Q,\text{wind}}, \gamma_{Q,\text{snow}}, \gamma_{Q,\text{imposed}})\) — or just \((\gamma_G, \gamma_Q)\) — is found by minimising

\[ M(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_S) = \sum_{k \in D} w_k \big(\beta_k(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_S) - \beta_t\big)^2 \]

with \(\beta_t = 3.8\), the EN 1990 target for a 50-year reference period (step 5 and 6 of the procedure). The material factors \(\gamma_{M,i}\) are kept at their Eurocode values throughout.
